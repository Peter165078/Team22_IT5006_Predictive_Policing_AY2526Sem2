Problem Framing
From event rows to district-hour cells

We model occurrence risk, not exact incidents

P(Y = 1 | X), where Y = 1 means at least one crime occurs in a district-hour cell

Why district-hour is our final setup

  • It creates natural positive and negative labels from the space-time grid.
  • It avoids the earlier workaround of copying event rows to synthesize negatives.
  • It is more operationally interpretable than record-level prediction.
  • It aligns cleanly with chronological evaluation and rolling history features.

What we explicitly do not claim

  • We do not predict the exact crime type of a future incident.
  • We do not automate enforcement decisions.
  • We do not interpret feature importance as causal evidence.
  • We present the system as a human-in-the-loop planning aid.
This reframing is the biggest methodological improvement in the final project: the label definition is cleaner, the evaluation setup is more defendable, and the story is easier to explain in 10 minutes.

Model training

Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and HistGradientBoosting.

Step 05
Evaluation + app

Metrics, feature importance, transfer testing, and the Streamlit interface.

Leakage control

  • Chronological split: train on 2015-2024, hold out 2025.
  • Rolling counts use only history before time T.
  • Post-incident fields are not used as predictors.
  • NIBRS transfer uses Chicago-fitted preprocessing statistics with passthrough spatial bounds.

What stays strong

Discrimination metrics remain meaningful outside Chicago, so the model is not learning only one city’s idiosyncrasies.

What weakens

How to present it
