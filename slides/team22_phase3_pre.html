Problem Definition
What exactly are we predicting?

Estimate
P(Y = 1 | X)

Given an observed feature vector X for a specific time-location instance, predict the probability that at least one crime incident occurs.

How we define the labels

  • Y = 1: at least one crime incident occurs in the space-time unit.
  • Y = 0: no recorded incident occurs in that space-time unit.
  • This is occurrence prediction, not crime-type classification.
  • The system is framed as a planning aid, not an autonomous enforcement tool.
We deliberately avoid overstating the task. The project aims to produce a modest, interpretable relative-risk signal that can support timing review and localized monitoring.

Dataset and Modeling Challenge
Source: yearly Chicago crime archives

What goes into the training set

  • Input data comes from yearly ZIP archives stored under apps/dashboard/split_data_by_year/.
  • A shared builder merges yearly archives into one modeling CSV.
  • The refactored default uses the last ten years ending in 2024 for model development.
  • The year 2025 is reserved as an explicit chronological holdout.

The hardest data issue

Event rows contain observed crimes only, so we refactored the task around explicit district-hour units.

In the refactored pipeline, each district-hour cell becomes one training example. Cells with incidents are labeled Y = 1, and cells with no incidents are labeled Y = 0. This gives us natural negatives without copying event rows.

This is one of the most important points for Q&A. The key idea is that the final model is trained on explicit space-time units, not on copied event rows with synthetic labels.

Feature Design
05
Pipeline Features Models Results
Temporal + Spatial + Historical
Feature Engineering Strategy
Designed to avoid leakage and preserve deployment realism

Temporal

  • hour, day_of_week, month, week_of_year
  • Cyclic encodings for hour, weekday, and month
  • Binary indicators for weekend, night, and rush hour

Spatial

  • District, ward, beat, and community area
  • Latitude / longitude and X-Y coordinates
  • Grid-cell binning and missingness indicators

Historical

  • crimes_last_7d, 14d, 30d, 90d
  • Local density proxy: crime_density_500m
  • Counts are computed strictly from events before time T

Leakage control

  • Post-incident fields such as Primary Type, IUCR, Arrest, and Domestic are dropped.
  • Chronological split uses 70% train, 15% validation, and 15% test.
  • Cold-start rows without prior history are removed after feature construction.

Why 7 / 14 / 30 / 90 days?

  • 7d for weekly rhythm
  • 14d for short-term carryover
  • 30d for monthly seasonality
  • 90d for quarterly persistence

Spatial / Temporal Alignment and Feature Importance
Important for understanding what the models actually learn

Spatial-temporal evaluation

  • Hourly correlation is strong for all models, often above 0.93.
  • District-level hotspot ranking is much weaker and sometimes even negatively correlated.
  • This means the models track when risk changes better than where hotspots rank.

Main interpretation

The current pipeline learns temporal rhythm far more strongly than precise spatial hotspot ranking. So the demo should be presented as a timing-aware planning aid, not a precise district ranking engine.

Conclusion and Q&A
Most likely questions to prepare for

What we built

  • A reproducible end-to-end pipeline from yearly archives to deployed demo
  • A binary crime occurrence prediction formulation with cautious label construction
  • A modest but consistent benchmark signal, led slightly by HistGradientBoosting
  • A Streamlit proof-of-concept for interactive prediction and discussion

Questions we expect

  • Why did we switch from event rows to district-hour units?
  • Why use chronological split instead of random split?
  • Why choose 7, 14, 30, and 90-day windows?
  • Why are model differences so small?
  • Why is temporal alignment stronger than spatial hotspot ranking?
Final framing: this is a decision-support proof-of-concept with clear methodological limitations, not a production policing system.
